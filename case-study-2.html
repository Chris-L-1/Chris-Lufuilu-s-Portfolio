Project Overview

This project involved designing a mobile application from concept to high-fidelity prototype. The app aimed to solve a real pain point for users who needed a simple, reliable way to track and manage a specific area of their daily life — with a focus on clarity, speed, and ease of use in real-world mobile contexts.

I led the complete design process: conducting initial user research, sketching concepts, building low and mid-fidelity wireframes, creating a component-based design system, and delivering a fully interactive high-fidelity prototype ready for developer handoff.

UI Design Mobile Design Prototyping Design System

The Problem

"Existing solutions are either too complex and overwhelming, or too simplified to actually be useful. Users want something that feels personal and effortless — not another app to manage."

Research showed that users had tried multiple existing apps but abandoned them all within the first week — not because the apps lacked features, but because they felt like tools rather than helpful companions. The opportunity was to design something that felt human, contextual, and genuinely easy to use.

Research Methods

Before touching Figma, I spent time understanding users and mapping the competitive landscape.

User Interviews

Spoke with 5 potential users to understand their current habits, frustrations, and expectations.

Competitor Analysis

Reviewed 6 competitor apps, mapping their UX patterns, strengths, and major gaps.

User Personas

Built 2 distinct personas from research data to keep design decisions anchored to real user needs.

Card Sorting

Used remote card sorting to validate how users mentally categorised features before building the navigation structure.

Key Insights

Research surfaced clear patterns that directly shaped every design decision:

01
Onboarding is make-or-break. Users who weren't immediately oriented during onboarding never came back. First impressions in mobile apps are disproportionately important.
02
Users want feedback, not features. Participants didn't ask for more functionality — they wanted to feel acknowledged and understood by the app. Micro-interactions and clear progress feedback were high priorities.
03
Speed matters more than completeness. Users wanted to accomplish the core action in under 10 seconds. Anything requiring more than 2 taps for the main task was seen as too slow.

Wireframes

I began with rapid sketching on paper — generating 20+ screen concepts in a single session — before moving to low-fidelity wireframes in Figma. This allowed me to explore structure and flow without getting distracted by visual details.

Final UI Design

The final high-fidelity prototype was built in Figma with a fully documented design system — including colour tokens, typography scale, component library, and interaction states. The design was tested with 3 users before finalisation.

The visual language is clean and minimal, using generous whitespace and a warm colour palette to create an experience that feels approachable rather than clinical. Every screen was designed mobile-first, optimised for one-handed use.

What I Learned

01

Start with constraints

Knowing your screen size, thumb reach zones, and typical connection speeds before designing saves major rework later.

02

Design systems scale

Building a component library first felt slow — but it made iterating on 20+ screens dramatically faster and more consistent.

03

Test early, test often

Getting feedback on wireframes prevented expensive changes at the high-fidelity stage. Even 2 test participants surface major issues.
