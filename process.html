How I Work

A process built
on listening.

Great design isn't a straight line, it's an iterative loop of questioning, understanding, building, and refining. Here's how I approach every project.

01

Empathy

Before I open Figma or draw a single line, I spend time with people. This stage is about immersing myself in the world of the users, understanding their context, motivations, frustrations, and goals without projecting my own assumptions onto them.

Empathy means genuinely trying to see the world through someone else's eyes. In South Africa, this often means being acutely aware of factors like data costs, device diversity, literacy levels, and the gap between how designers think and how everyday users experience technology.

Shadowing Stakeholder Interviews Empathy Maps Diary Studies
02

Research

Structured research turns curiosity into evidence. I select methods based on what we need to know, not based on habit or convenience. Sometimes that means in-depth interviews; sometimes it means a quick guerrilla usability test or desk research.

The goal is to gather enough evidence to make confident design decisions, while staying lean enough not to delay the work unnecessarily. Good research asks the right questions, not every question.

User Interviews Surveys Competitor Analysis Analytics Review Desk Research
03

Synthesis & Define

Raw data doesn't solve problems, insights do. This stage is about making sense of everything I've learned: clustering observations, finding patterns, and extracting the key insights that should drive design decisions.

By the end of this phase, I have a clear problem statement, a set of user personas, and a prioritised list of insights that will guide every decision forward. This is where assumptions are replaced by evidence.

Affinity Mapping User Personas Journey Maps HMW Statements Problem Framing
04

Ideate

This is the creative explosion phase, quantity over quality, no bad ideas. I use sketching, crazy eights, storyboarding, and collaborative workshops to generate a wide range of possible solutions before narrowing down.

Divergent thinking creates possibility. The best final designs almost never come from the first idea. I push myself to explore at least 3–5 distinct directions before committing to a path forward.

Crazy Eights Sketching Storyboarding Mind Mapping Design Sprints
05

Prototype

I build prototypes at the lowest fidelity that will answer my current questions. Paper prototypes test navigation and structure. Figma wireframes test layout and hierarchy. High-fidelity prototypes test visual design and interaction detail.

The goal is never perfection at this stage, it's learning. A prototype is a tool for having a conversation with users, not a final product. I prototype to discover problems, not to validate assumptions.

Paper Prototyping Wireframes Figma Prototyping Interactive Mockups Design Systems
06

Test & Iterate

Testing with real users is non-negotiable. I conduct moderated and unmoderated usability tests, gather qualitative and quantitative feedback, and use what I learn to improve the design in rapid iteration cycles.

Design is never truly finished, it's released. But each iteration brings it closer to genuinely serving users' needs. Even 3–5 test participants can surface the most critical issues. The goal is always to learn faster than the competition.
